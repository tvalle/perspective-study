Rules of thumb
  • The horizon is your eye level. It is the vanishing line of every horizontal plane (floor, table tops, box tops). Only the viewer changes it — boxes never do.
  • Yaw (turning a box around the vertical) slides its two VPs along the horizon. It never creates a new line.
  • Parallel boxes share VPs, wherever they sit — same row, deeper, higher, floating.
  • Every plane has its own vanishing line. Tilting a box (pitch or roll) tilts its faces, so their vanishing lines leave the horizon — a "new horizon" for that surface (ramps, roofs, open lids, a book tipped up).
  • Tilting around a horizontal hinge: the hinge's VP stays on the horizon and the new line pivots around it.
  • Camera tilt (looking up/down) moves the horizon in the frame and adds a vertical VP (3-point). Camera roll only rotates the horizon in the frame. Camera height changes how much top/underside you see.
